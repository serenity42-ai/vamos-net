# Asunción P2 2026: Chingalán Closes the Gap, Bea and Paula Take the Lead

Alejandro Galán and Federico Chingotto beat Arturo Coello and Agustín Tapia 6-3, 7-5 in the men's final at the Ueno COP Arena, their fourth title of 2026 and a third win over the Golden Boys in their four meetings this season. Brussels denied the world its "classic" two weeks ago when Lebrón and Augsburger broke up the bracket; Asunción gave it back, and Chingalán closed it with two consecutive winners off the side wall at match point. On the women's side, Beatriz González and Paula Josemaría beat world No. 1s Gemma Triay and Delfina Brea 4-6, 6-3, 6-3 for their fourth consecutive title, and finally took the Race lead — by all of 40 points. The 2026 season has its two best pairs in each draw, and at the end of Paraguay both of them lost.

The men's final ran on Court Central in front of a packed Ueno COP Arena. Chingotto and Galán came in with three more hours on court than their rivals — a marathon 4-6, 7-6(5), 7-6(4) quarterfinal against Maxi Arce and Juan Tello had cost them most of a working day — and started the way fatigued favorites usually don't, with an immediate break in the opening game. Coello and Tapia broke straight back. The match flipped for good in the fifth game of set 1, when Chingalán broke a second time and rode it to a 6-3 manga. Set 2 turned into a hold-fest until game 12, where Chingotto and Galán earned two championship points on the Coello-Tapia serve. The Golden Boys saved both. Then Chingotto, who has spent 2026 quietly becoming the most clutch right-court player on tour, ended it with two side-wall winners back-to-back. Title; manga 7-5. *"Campeones en Paraguay,"* Galán wrote on Instagram afterwards. *"Feliz por la semana y el trabajo, gracias equipo."* The H2H this season agrees with the result: it's 3-1 Chingalán in 2026, even though the lifetime balance is still 21-12 for Coello and Tapia. With this win Galán moves to **57 career titles**, passing Tapia (55) for the most among active players. He's also continuing to pull away from his own former half: two years on from the March 2024 split, the player Lebrón helped make is now the most-decorated man on the tour, full stop.

The path to the final says a lot about how Chingalán built their season. Seeded second, they entered at the round of 16 and didn't drop a set: 7-6(3), 6-4 over Iñigo Jofre and David Gala in a tighter R16 than the score suggests, then three hours of cardio against Arce and Tello in the quarters, then 6-1, 7-5 in a semifinal against tournament revelation Aimar Goñi and Edu Alonso — Goñi's first Premier Padel semifinal at 20, after the Navarrese beat Stupaczuk and Yanguas in the quarters. Chingalán now stand at **24-3 on the season together**. They are the only major men's pair on tour that survived the off-season intact, and the pair that has now beaten the Golden Boys three times in four meetings this year. On the other side of the bracket, Coello and Tapia made what looked like one of their cleanest weeks since the Qatar Major: 6-4, 6-4 in the R16 against Bautista and Campagnolo, a 6-1, 6-0 quarterfinal against Coki Nieto and Jon Sanz that lasted barely an hour, and then a brutal 6-2, 6-1 semifinal demolition of Lebrón and Augsburger that vacated whatever runway Brussels had given the Andalusian. Three of the four men's semifinalist pairs in Asunción were formed in the last six months. Chingalán is the only one that wasn't, and they won.

The women's final was the **fifth straight meeting** between Triay/Brea and González/Josemaría, and the fourth straight win for Bea and Paula. It was also Gemma Triay's **101st career final** — four short of Alejandra Salazar's all-time record of 105 — and her and Brea's **11th consecutive final** of 2026 — they haven't missed one all year. Bea and Paula broke first, in game five of set 1, and built a 4-2 lead with Bea controlling the trade-speed and Paula reading the bandeja angles. Triay and Brea broke back to 4-4 and again in the closing game to steal the set 6-4. Set 2 reversed the pattern: an immediate break for 2-0, a steady push to 2-5, a Triay/Brea break-back to 5-3, and then a final break to close 6-3. Set 3 turned for good at 1-3, where Bea and Paula took a third consecutive break, started taking time away from Triay's transitions and quieted Brea's offense. The No. 1s saved **seven match points** before finally yielding the 6-3. *"La perla de El Palo también es la perla de Asunción,"* the Spanish press called Bea this week — she's now won this tournament three years running, with three different partners (Brea in 2024, Claudia Fernández in 2025, Josemaría in 2026). Coach Claudio Gilardoni gets the structural credit; the pair was a question mark at the start of the year and is now the only women's duo to win four trophies in a row in 2026. With Asunción, González and Josemaría also flip the Race for the first time, leading Triay and Brea 3,520 to 3,480. Forty points. One quarterfinal swings it.

The tour now heads to Buenos Aires for the P1, where both world No. 1 pairs are defending last year's title and the Race standings could turn over again inside a week. Bea and Paula's 40-point cushion is the kind of margin that disappears between rounds. Chingalán's nearly 400-point lead over Coello and Tapia looks more comfortable until you remember the men's draw moves a hemisphere south and the home crowd will be Argentine. Two weeks after that, the Rome Major — where Triay and Brea defend the women's title. The race for 2026 isn't open anymore. It's contested, on both sides, by exactly the four pairs everyone hoped for at the start of the year, and as of this weekend none of them are the team to beat.
